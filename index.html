Why GTP

Today's clocks were designed for a world that moved at the speed of trains. We now work, travel and talk across the whole planet in seconds, and the old system shows its age.

The problem with time today

  • The same moment has dozens of different names, one for every time zone.
  • Clocks jump forward and back for daylight saving, on different dates in different countries.
  • A day is counted in 24, then 60, then 60, so even simple sums need care.
  • Every international plan starts with the question "your time or mine?"

What GTP does about it

  • Every moment gets a single timestamp that is the same everywhere on Earth.
  • There are no zones to convert and no seasonal clock changes.
  • The day is counted in hundreds, so time adds and subtracts like ordinary numbers.
  • The date and the time sit together in one short line.

What makes GTP different

A GTP timestamp reads from largest to smallest: the year, the day of the year, then three levels of one hundred.

  • One reading for everyone

    At any moment the GTP time is identical in Chennai, London and New York. Nothing changes when you cross a border.

  • A day in hundreds

    A day has 100 Orah, an Orah has 100 Epto, and an Epto has 100 Tick. Half a day is simply 50 Orah.

  • Date and time together

    The day of the year is part of the timestamp, so one line says exactly when, with no month names or formats to argue over.

  • Works with the clocks you have

    GTP converts exactly to and from local time, so you can use both side by side while the world catches up.

Where GTP is useful

Anywhere people or machines in different places need to agree on a moment.

  • Meetings across countries

    Send one GTP time and everyone reads the same number, with no working out who is ahead or behind.

  • Travel and transport

    Flights, ships and trains cross time zones all day. One time on every ticket and timetable removes the conversions.

  • Computer systems and data

    Servers in different countries can stamp every record with the same clock, so events line up in order.

  • Markets and trading

    Exchanges open and close in many zones. A shared time makes deadlines and settlement times plain to every side.

  • Live events and launches

    A broadcast, a game release or a sale can start at one announced time that means the same thing to every viewer.

  • Science and space

    Experiments and missions already need a time that does not depend on a place. A day in hundreds also makes the sums simple.

The future of time is one time.

Time zones were a nineteenth-century answer to the railway. They served well for over a hundred years, but a connected world needs a clock it can share.

Our ambition is for GTP to become the way the world keeps time: on phones and watches, in timetables and calendars, and inside the networks that run everything else. We see it as a revolution in timekeeping, and it starts with one app.

Survey app: every answer from a real, unique person

Our survey app is built around one idea: each person is verified once, so brands hear from real people and people are rewarded for honest answers. The name will be announced at launch.

Why a verified survey app

Online surveys are only as good as the people answering them. Fake accounts and repeat entries waste money and give brands the wrong picture.

How it works

  1. Verify once. An age check, a private face scan, phone and email. One person, one account.
  2. Answer, try, speak up. Surveys, free product samples and complaints, in 11 languages.
  3. Earn. Rewards go to the person's own UPI ID.

What makes it different

  • No duplicate respondents: one face, one account.
  • Samples reach real people, and cannot be claimed twice.
  • Complaints come from verified buyers, with a clear reply deadline for the brand.
  • Privacy by design: brands see answers, not identities, unless the person agrees.
  • Every record carries GTP time, shown next to local time.

Where it is useful

For any company that needs to hear from real customers, and for people who want their opinion to count.

  • Market research for brands

    Ask a question and know that each answer came from a different, real person who matches the profile you asked for.

  • Product sampling

    Hand out free samples and see that each one reached a verified person, once, with their opinion coming back.

  • Complaints that get answered

    A customer photographs the product, the app writes up the complaint, and the brand hears from a real buyer with a reply deadline.
